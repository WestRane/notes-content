## Beyond Barns and Stables
*July 2026*

Whereas the first season tried its best to balance between agricultural mundanity, classical slice of life and grounded drama mostly covering the livestock, the show's second outing takes the characters' tragedies much deeper. The pigs aren't the targeted entities anymore - humans are. Let's talk about how the development is super reasonable in this yet another deceptive season.

### Hachiken's Continual Recovery

The show ends with Hachiken saying "I'm not the same person as I was before" to his mom. That's basically the result of what we saw throughout these two seasons. That's why we're all here. But the process of his recovery is sophisticated and involved.

Yuugo starts with the inability to feel himself safe amongst his peers and not being able to fulfill his parents' expectations. He drops off the competition and runs away to the agricultural high school, thinking this might shake up his life or at least make it less grueling. This season, as already specified, expands its field of view, taking characters into account much further than before. And by that... well, the writers make Hachiken stick his nose into personal matters of Komaba and Mikage.

#### Reasons to Help

At first it seems like Yuugo should have some sort of a curiosity limit, because being overly curios about other people's problems does not mean it's okay to ask again and again, hoping they would decide to reveal the truth to you. Hachiken imposes himself on them really hard, trying to sniff out the secrets between those too, but it feels cringy and awkward, though illustrating a classical teenage jealousy of a girl. That being said, as the show moves forward, this behavior starts to take a more precise and genuine shape, although making it barely less emotional.

It might seem like this somewhat nasty behavior would only alienate his closest friends, but it's not the case here at all. The pupils of a pretty niche school with a low entrance bar are expected to be less complex in the way they think and interact. Is this true for this show? No, except for a small comedy off-topic stuff. Mikage and Komaba both note his behavior. Mikage tries to push it off, while Komaba is being more direct and explicitly asks Hachiken not to stick his nose. Is that cruel? No, not at all.

The reason Yuugo's friends try not to involve one into their problems is rather apparent. They just don't want him to forget about his own struggles by caring about others too much. But the writers take a clever step by making Hachiken suffer nonetheless. With that in mind - and we'll discuss that suffer later - it's needless to say the outcome couldn't have been low-quality. It's the author of Fullmetal Alchemist we're talking about, come on.

Hachiken has his own angle on how he sees his friends and acts toward them. It finally becomes revealed, and in a more explicit manner at that. It's not just an inability to care about himself more than of the others, but is rather an endless cycle of tries to support them regardless of the actual lay of the land. When there's nothing to do concerning Komaba's ranch, not only is Hachiken bent on thinking of a solution, but he also deeply cares about Ichirou and Aki alike. That's probably the first time he states that his desire isn't about making him responsible for fixing other people's stuff, but is about helping his friends in any possible way as it makes everyone happier. And that obviously includes cheering them up or splitting the sorrow with them.

That's the best Hachiken could've gotten from a niche school. Friends who care about him and make him feel valuable despite lacking dreams and specific plans for the future. He doesn't have his own dreams, so he entirely reasonably spends his time caring about others' dreams.

And what's even better, he finally is able to feel the mutual support of the same kind from his somewhat beloved ones in reply to him opening up.

#### Illness of Overworking

Overtime work isn't a new concept at all, and things were hardly any better back in 2014, but it's not like it becomes bad on its own. It becomes bad when the writers make it cliched and impersonal. And whilst the events of the show are usually presented in a slice-of-life style, at a steady pace, the arc of overworking uses a completely different approach. As it turns out, Gin no Saji can try to force the viewers to feel bad for the people as well. Hachiken is worried about Mikage's future, the festival's success, training the dog, operating the class budget and so many other things, so it's absolutely inevitable the writers would play that card and make him suffer a lot. And this is exactly what happens.

This definitely is not a fresh move, but it's a well-made one nevertheless. Most anime depict this topic by forcing characters to have as lot of work to do as possible, as if in an endless conveyor belt. Gin no Saji takes it differently. Instead, they rely on balance, showing both the work stuff and the ways for Hachiken to build his personal life. It obviously means he manages to invite Mikage on a date mid preparations for the festival. This arc, despite being only two episodes, manages to both develop relationships and finally reveal main character's father.

It's also interesting to see the illustration of a completely natural teenage reaction with less reasoning when emotions cloud judgment. Hachiken meets face to face with his distrust of the friends and, on the other hand, their completely reasonable and natural perspective. "We can't leave our festival posts during the day, and the hospital doesn't allow visitors at night", and also "We knew how hard you'd been working for this, so we wanted to make it work, no matter what". 

The last chord (the note actually) saying "I'd been kind of down lately, but seeing the horse working so hard to draw the sled cheered me up. Thank you" is genuinely the most natural note to make Hachiken cry. It's neither the pretentious nor the cute one, it's that calm, restrained, but genuine, honest and meaningful one. No need to get a high score in school. No need to hear his parent's approval and dry praise. A simple gratitude to his efforts to make a horse pull a big chunk of painted metal is enough.

### Groundness of Komaba's Ranch

It's really satisfying - in a weird way - to see Komaba's ranch being sold off. Yeah, it sounds harsh, just like the drama around Pork Bowl was, but the problem is **unsolvable** and that's the right thing to show to the viewers. That's the thing! In a sense, it's a marvelous addition, as, yeah, dairy science, livestock care and other manifestations of that culture are rough.

Not every student has their dream come true, and some of them are genuinely forced to work despite only being high school students. Farms are being closed, animals you've been raising all of your life are being sent to the slaughterhouse, and there's nothing you can do.

What makes this story feel softer is that Ichirou really tried his best to help with the debts while also studying like any normal student. It is realistic, grounded, and that's what makes this story even more breathtaking. As for the outcome, the writers don't make much of a drama afterward. Komaba... works. He gets his salary, jumps on his bike and drives safely home. It's a life, is what the author is trying to say. Things happen. You could just grab the last ranch's milk and share it with your friends, smiling despite the sorrow. Very grounded.

### Mikage's Honesty

That is a part of Hachiken's growth as well, but Mikage manages to be so much more honest with her parents and with Yuugo himself. At first, she expresses her gratitude and appreciation for Hachiken "crossing the line" and letting her tell him about career paths during the summer break. The original dialogue in the previous season was awkward, but also intense in the sense of character feelings and development. Hachiken ran away from his harsh studies, so having seen Mikage giving in to her family's desires while dropping her own aspirations, it's only natural to see Yuugo being emotional and telling her to talk to the parents.

That being said, it doesn't mean the conflict get resolved as soon as she states her worries. The relationship between these two isn't simple as well. When Hachiken struggles to succeed with Maron, he fallbacks to his default setting, thus willing to go for practicing on the horse a lot more. Mikage shouts at him: "Horses also need rest! If they become exhausted and get sick, they may even need to be put down!". It's a classical Gin no Saji conflict. Hachiken only knows the way to become better by putting even more effort, time, sweat and blood, but Mikage shows her care about livestock and horses, even if it means to scold Yuugo.

Aki's decision to talk to her parents and reveal her dreams is yet another addition to the collection of endearing things of this show. Parents care about you.

### Parents of the Main Character

But not every parent show their love to the child flawlessly.

Hachiken's mother proves to be moderately adequate. She lied to Hachiken about father liking his bacon, but only to make Yuugo feel better. Meanwhile she wanted to hear the same thing toward her cuisine as well. What seems odd at first makes a significant shift, making the mother less of an unknown character and more of an another victim of her husband's lack of emotions. She gets surprised by someone saying her food is tasty when Hachiken's at their place.

And it's not about emotions only. It's about Kazumasa's emotional intelligence as it is. I'm not familiar with the events of the original manga, but these two seasons simply don't reveal this character by any means except for "cruel, strict parent". Needless to say, it's still much better than some anime that love to justify such profiles, because Gin no Saji doesn't make such an attempt mostly. They say "your parents made sure you guys had good food growing up", which could be interpreted as justifying, but I see this more as a reason for Yuugo to at least give it a chance and listen to the parents calmly.

He does exactly that. "Make an effort to understand him," he thinks to himself. Poor boy, if only it wasn't for naught. The father obviously values studies, money, effort, top ranks, doing your best, lack of emotions and stuff, meanwhile the series is about cooperation, friendship, kindness, appreciation for the small mundane things. The writers use him as an instrument to show the contrast, but his behavior isn't reasonable enough. We see a disappointed father who has never been kind to his son, thus raising an enormous amount of complexes and problems in Yuugo's life. And I consider it the show's flaw, because the character becomes too one-dimensional and acts like a antagonist of a shounen rather than a grounded person with his own perspective, even if it's a questionable one. Hachiken's being so much more reasonable, comparing himself to livestock and criticizing the aforementioned concepts.

It feels like the writers could've said more about that, but perhaps they didn't due to time constraints. The archetype of a japanese worker driven purely by performance might be grounded, and the fact that he's at fault for Hachiken's life avalanche isn't particularly a flaw of the show (it's the character's one), but it's blatantly underdeveloped.

### The Result

The second season is still as fun as ever, while also being deep and having underlying character's struggles revealed.

Mikage stops being scared of having a dream yet not working toward it and lying to herself for the rest of her life, because she starts moving toward making the dream come true. Hachiken gains even more emotional intelligence and rational thinking, being more gentle to his mother and being hundred times less restrained toward his father. Komaba's life isn't destroyed despite harsh reality stomping on him. Hachiken's family becomes closer to each other, and his father... well, forces any viewer to sigh deeply.

Neverthless, there's romance, and there's struggles, and there's joy and kindness. And this show makes these soft, sweet and genuine feelings appear as if out of thin air. The world is cruel, but there's always someone who can place a hand on your shoulder and reassure. It's one of the brightest feelings amidst all.
